A note from the developer

I was looking for work.
Then I started building this.

I'm Joshua Jenks. After years working in technical support and systems, I found myself doing the same job-application work again and again. Every hiring site wanted my résumé, my history, and another version of an answer I'd already written.

I started YourMove because I wanted that work to count for more than one application.

Read the story Ways to help
“I already told you all this.”

It's a reasonable thing to think after uploading your résumé and being asked to type it out again.

One applicant's frustration. An open-source response.

The application shouldn't forget everything you've done.

Looking for work already takes time. You research the company, decide whether the role fits, and work out how to explain the experience that makes you qualified. Then you open another hiring platform and start from an empty form.

I'd already written useful answers and documented years of work. What I didn't have was a practical way to bring that information into the next application without doing it all over again.

YourMove keeps job details, prepared résumés, approved career information, and reusable answers close at hand. For supported employer forms, it can help prepare information for review. The applicant still decides what to use and personally submits on the employer's site.

Supporting independent development

If you'd like to help, here's what it supports.

YourMove is an independent, open-source project. Contributions give me more time to work on the parts that make it useful for real applicants.

Work through real applications

Find the friction in actual job-seeking workflows, not hypothetical examples.

Make supported forms more reliable

Test where preparation works, identify where it fails, and improve what applicants can recover.

Get a usable desktop release into people's hands

Finish installation, distribution, and practical release checks without overstating readiness.

Support YourMove

One-time or monthly contributions go through Ko-fi. You don't need to contribute to use or follow the project.

Support on Ko-fi ↗ Entirely optional. Not a charitable donation. Contributions don't buy product access, special treatment, or influence over development.

If you're job hunting, keep your money.

Sharing the project, reporting what doesn't work, or telling me what would actually help you is useful too. I'd rather build something people can use than make anyone feel obligated to pay for it.
